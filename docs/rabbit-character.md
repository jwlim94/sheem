# Rabbit character

Status: V8 appearance approved by the user on 2026-09-30. The user selected the rabbit from the
[human/rabbit concept](content/assets/characters/human-rabbit-concepts-v1.png).
V9 adds a local in-place walk study while preserving the V8 appearance.
This is an increment toward the next character-focused Short, not completion of
M1 locomotion or the audio MVP.

## Look and deliverables

Warm oatmeal body, subtle cheek fullness, upright peach-lined ears, sage kerchief, apricot
crossbody satchel and a round tail. Proportions and accessories follow the concept;
the geometry is a first interpretation for review, not an exact reconstruction.

- [Browser GLB](../public/models/rabbit/sheem-rabbit-v14.glb): 833,024 bytes, 5,268
  triangles, two material primitives, thirteen bones, a four-second `Idle` and
  0.8-second `Walk` and `Run` clips. One embedded 768×768 face texture; no external texture or decoder requests.
- [Editable Blender source](../art/characters/rabbit/sheem-rabbit-v14.blend).
- [Rebuild script](../art/characters/rabbit/build_rabbit.py), tested in Blender 4.4.1.
- [Studio render](../art/characters/rabbit/rabbit-preview-v14.png).

The model was authored locally from the in-session generated concept, using
procedural mesh construction in Blender. No third-party character mesh or texture
was downloaded. The script is the reproducible source; rebuilding overwrites the
named `.blend`, `.glb` and preview PNG. Save hand-edited variants separately.

```sh
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender --background --factory-startup --python art/characters/rabbit/build_rabbit.py
npm run dev
```

Open `/playground/rabbit`. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, choose front/side/back,
switch between Studio and Meadow, select Idle/Walk/Run, or pause playback. Reduced-motion preference
starts the preview paused. The product's `/` route retains its existing title and
entry behavior; the rabbit study is loaded only on its own route.

## Face revision

The initial raised eye meshes looked like buttons from the side. V2 paints the
eyes, highlights, eyebrows and smile directly onto the head using front-projected
UVs. The raised muzzle patch is removed; cheek and muzzle tint are softly painted.
Eyes are slightly smaller and matte. Only the nose retains very shallow relief
(about 6 mm at avatar scale). Back polygons sample plain fur, so the expression
does not appear on the back of the head.

The texture is generated by the modeling script, packed into both Blender and
GLB, and saved as `art/characters/rabbit/rabbit-face-v2.png` for inspection. V1
model files are retained for comparison; the preview and download now use V14.

## Cheek revision

V3 narrows the upper skull and adds continuous lower-cheek fullness below the
eyes. The swelling wraps around both sides so the rear silhouette also has fuller
lower corners, with a tapered chin. Extra head topology keeps the cheek outline
rounded. No separate cheek spheres are attached. The painted V2 expression and
body proportions are retained. Front, side and back inspection is available in
the preview; earlier GLBs are retained for comparison.

V4 corrects the pointed lower corners seen in V3. It replaces the narrow
displacement band with smoothly fused ellipsoidal skull/cheek volumes. The cheek
outline now has a rounded turn into the chin in front and rear views, while the
painted expression remains unchanged. That iteration exported V4; older model
versions remain available for comparison.

V5 responds to the overly prominent side and rear lobes in V4. It returns to a
single oval skull with only a broad, shallow front-only swelling (up to 4.5%
lateral scaling and 0.010 Blender units forward). The rear stays an undeformed
ellipsoid, without separate cheek volumes. Face paint, accessories and rig remain
the same. That iteration used V5.

V6 restores V3's fuller lower-cheek silhouette after V5 removed too much volume.
The original vertical swelling is broadened from 0.17 to 0.265 Blender units,
with lateral amplitude close to V3 (0.125 versus 0.135), rounding the widest turn
rather than replacing the head with an oval or separate cheek volumes. Depth
swelling is reduced to 2.5% to keep the back continuous. That iteration used V6.

V7 makes a small correction to V6: lateral cheek displacement drops from 0.125
to 0.090 (28% less added swelling, not 28% less head width). The rounded vertical
profile, skull, depth, face and body are retained. That iteration used V7.

V8 trims lateral cheek displacement one more small step, from 0.090 to 0.075.
The vertical rounding and all other proportions stay as in V7. That iteration used V8.

## Walk study (V9)

The preview now offers Idle, Walk and Pause/Play. Switching while playing blends
poses over 0.25 seconds; choosing a clip while paused displays its starting pose.
Reduced-motion preference starts paused. The accepted V8 geometry is retained.
Two independent foot bones keep soles level and a bag bone adds restrained sway.
Arms counter-swing; body, ears, head and tail have small secondary movements.

The in-place cycle lasts 1.6 seconds. Feet alternate a 0.8-second planted phase
and eased return, with 4.5 cm maximum lift and 20 cm stance travel. A future
controller should match approximately 0.25 m/s at normal playback speed to cancel
stance sliding in world space. Root translation stays zero. This is a stylized
rigid-part gait on flat ground, not terrain IK, keyboard locomotion or footstep audio.

Validation: browser-loaded GLB has both clips at their expected durations. Sampled
sole height stays above the studio floor (minimum 0.0002 m), stance height is
constant and the loop closes. Chrome verified animated front/side views, repeated
Idle/Walk switching, stable paused frames, paused clip selection, reduced-motion
startup and a 390×844 layout with no horizontal overflow or runtime exceptions.

### Walking speed adjustment (V10)

The user found the initial 0.35 m/s movement too slow. V10 doubles movement to
0.70 m/s and extends stance travel from 20 to 28 cm, keeping the 1.6-second clip.
Its nominal gait speed is therefore 0.35 m/s and full movement uses 2× playback,
rather than doubling the old cadence to 2.8×. Grounded-foot speed stays matched to
movement. Browser sampling verified 0.70 m/s movement and 0.35 m/s nominal gait,
with a closed loop and unchanged minimum sole height of 0.0002 m. The same shape,
controls, acceleration, camera and bounds are retained.

### Shift running (V11)

Holding either Shift key while moving requests Run; releasing both returns to
Walk. Shift by itself does not move the avatar. The touch pad also has a hold-to-run
button. The model preview exposes a separate Run clip. Running has a forward lean,
shorter foot contact, higher swing clearance, stronger arm swing and secondary ear /
bag movement. Its 0.8-second cycle covers 40 cm during 0.32-second stance, giving a
nominal 1.25 m/s gait. Root translation remains zero. Movement chooses clips from
actual speed with a small hysteresis band and fades over 0.18 seconds.

Walk speed increases from 0.70 to 0.90 m/s; Run uses 1.80 m/s. Diagonal normalization,
bounds, blur/visibility cleanup, terrain following and camera remain shared.
Shift does not consume browser default behavior, so Shift+Tab still works.

V11 validation: browser input events with fixed-step updates measured 1.80 m/s

### Upright, energetic movement (V12)

Following visual feedback, normal movement now uses the previous sprint speed,
1.80 m/s; Shift runs at 3.00 m/s. Run keeps the torso nearly upright (about two
degrees of forward lean), swings opposing arms through a larger arc, raises each
swing foot 14 cm and includes two brief flight phases per cycle. Root motion is
vertical only; the controller still owns travel. Thigh length follows the ankle
target so the wider stride does not separate the feet from the legs.

Walk covers 44 cm in a 0.4-second stance; Run covers 60 cm in 0.28 seconds.
Both source clips last 0.8 seconds. Their travel-matched playback rates are about
1.64× and 1.40× respectively, avoiding the previous short-stride shuffle. Earlier
V9–V11 assets remain available for comparison. Acceptance: orbit to the side in
Run preview, then compare normal movement and Shift in the meadow; the upright
pose, larger arm swing and flight should make the two modes visibly distinct.
This comparison is also a candidate for the next character-movement Short.

V12 checks: rendered side views at contact, swing and flight; sampled Run soles

## Playable meadow study

Open `/playground/rabbit/walk`, or choose **Walk in the meadow** from the model
preview. WASD/arrow keys and on-screen direction buttons move relative to the
camera; holding Shift runs, dragging orbits and scrolling zooms. Back to start resets the rabbit.
The existing title screen and earlier experiments remain separate.

The avatar walks at 1.80 m/s and runs at 3.00 m/s with normalized diagonals and short acceleration /
deceleration. Animation playback follows actual speed (about 1.71× for Walk, 1.46× for Run), blending
back to Idle when stationary or blocked. A 5.5 m radius bounds a grass clearing
centered at (-9, -25), away from river/tree obstacles. Terrain height uses the same
triangle interpolation as the rendered meadow; the whole avatar follows its local
slope. Camera and close shadow light follow the player. There is no individual-foot
IK, object collision system, sound integration or multiplayer in this study.

Keyboard state clears on blur, hidden document and unmount. Pointer capture clears
held touch controls on release/cancel. Movement, camera and animation state are
owned outside React frame renders. A later character can reuse these controls once
its clips and scale are compatible. Extra gestures should follow acceptance of this
movement baseline.

Movement verification: normalized diagonal distance matched straight travel;
At the original speed, 60/120 Hz distance differed by about 1.4 mm over three seconds. Bounds stop at
5.5 m and terrain height matches the shared sampler. Browser input events verified
movement, heading, camera following, key-release stop, blur stop and reset; the
390×844 layout had no horizontal overflow or runtime exceptions. Software WebGL
was too slow for a useful real-time performance measurement, so movement assertions
used fixed-step R3F callbacks after input events, with actual rendered screenshots.
Physical-device frame rate and touch gestures remain unverified. Lint/build pass.

## Runtime contract

GLB uses +Y up, +Z forward, feet at approximately zero and a height of 1.70 m
including ears. This is a provisional avatar scale, not the size of a real rabbit.
`RabbitModel` clones skeletons per instance and owns its animation mixer; cached
geometry and vertex-painted materials remain shared. Frame updates do not render
React. Mixer actions/bindings are cleaned up on unmount.

The mesh contains intersecting stylized parts with rigid bone weights. It has
Idle, Walk and Run clips, but not a production deformation rig, facial rig, blink
system or terrain IK. The separate meadow study supplies a bounded movement controller.

The Meadow option reuses the title environment with an eight-meter grass clearing
around the subject so close grass does not obstruct inspection. This is a preview
arrangement, not a change to the title's terrain or a walkable spawn specification.
