**Transition into chapter 1 (built 2026-10-11): the rewind.** The scroll is 400dvh of room story plus 280dvh of rewind, with a brake between them on the welcome, the front shot of him waving (`RoomPortfolio.tsx`). On a trackpad, whose momentum keeps sending wheel events after the fingers lift, a new swipe made while the last still coasts counts as fresh input when its deltas rise again (momentum only decays), and a zero-delta event at a brake is held too (the browser lets a gesture be held only if its first event is): before (2026-10-14), a trackpad had to click to stop the momentum before the ring would fill. There is a second brake in chapter 1 (`HOLDS` in `story.ts`), at the front shot of him in his seat with the chapter's card up (school progress 0.52), where the school is told. Both work the same way; the ring is shared and shows the one being held. Scrolling down onto it stops dead there, however fast. The scroll is held at the room's end (`ROOM_END`); a wheel step that would carry past stops exactly on it; and the input that ran into it is swallowed until it pauses for 180 ms, so a flick's or a trackpad's momentum dies there. Fresh scrolling (wheel, touch, arrow and page keys) then fills a small ring at the bottom (`.room-hold`), at most 120 px per event, 700 px in all, about seven notches of a mouse wheel. Once full, the scroll is let go into the rewind. Scrolling up empties it, and going half a screen back above re-arms it. A big jump with no scrolling input (scrollbar, restored position) passes through. A first version gave the hold 60dvh of track, which a fast scroll flew straight through. Pauses in the input are measured on the events' own timestamps, and the first 350 ms after it bites are always swallowed. When the hall's first render blocked the page, a flick's queued events arrived late, read as a pause, and filled the ring at once. Scrolling on without a pause for over 2.5 s counts as deliberate and fills it. The exposure also lifts (1.05 → 1.25) over the welcome and eases back as he turns away. `story.ts` (`ROOM_SPLIT`, `rewindBeats`) holds the timings shared by the camera, clock, year counter, captions and audio. The steps run in order:

1. The welcome fades, the wave settles to idle, and he turns to face the wall clock (90 % of the way).
2. The camera glides over his head to the clock. It follows centripetal Catmull-Rom curves for the eye and the aim, sampled by arc length, with a slight roll in the middle. The clock sits in the left third of the frame on wide screens and in the centre on tall ones.
3. The clock hands run back three days, and the second hand hides so it does not strobe. A big year counter flips from the viewer's current year (2026 at least; it was 2025 until 2026-10-12, a mistake) back to 2019 like a mechanical drum (rest, quick flip, motion blur, soft mask), settling together with the hands.
4. The camera dives into the dial while the lens widens (36° to about 74°). The dial's cream then washes the screen.
5. The year turns from cream to ink, moves to the centre and heads the chapter 1 title card. The school scene itself is still to be built.

**Sound:** a low-pass on the master closes to about 400 Hz while time runs back, so the sound goes muffled, and reopens on the card. The wall clock is the rewind's sound (`RoomAudio.setClock`, 2026-10-12). The tick is "mab-clock-tick-1-20080713" by mab (Freesound #57211, CC BY 3.0, credited on the page): one tick of a wind-up travel alarm clock, a woody mid-range tock centred near 2.3 kHz. `art/portfolio-room/audio/build_tick.py` trims it to `tick.wav`, 103 ms, as a WAV so that no encoder delay sits before the click. It is pitched a semitone down, with a touch of weight near 700 Hz and a small room. A wristwatch tick (DrMinky #174721) was tried first; it was thin and high (near 6.7 kHz) even five semitones down. It runs on its own path, outside the master muffle, so it stays clear while the music goes under.
  - **At rest:** the room clock's second hand is now a quartz movement. It steps once a second with a tiny overshoot (it used to sweep, and sweeping clocks are silent). The tick is scheduled on each whole second, which is when the hand steps.
  - **Fading in and out:** it comes in as he turns to the clock (`look`). In the dive it goes dull (its own low-pass closes to 500 Hz) and away by the cream flash.
  - **While the hands run back:** it ticks each time the hour hand passes an hour mark (72 in all), so it speeds up and slows down with the hands. It is never more than about 13 ticks a second (`SPIN_GAP` 75 ms, jittered ±20 %), and marks passed faster are let go. It is softer the faster the hands go (to 45 % level). The first version allowed 40+ a second, reversed: an even stream that fast fused into a harsh, error-like buzz, and the reversed tick's sharp cut made it rasp.
  - Verified in Chrome by logging scheduled starts: rest ticks land within 5 ms of each whole second, and a fast scroll through the spin gives at most 13 ticks a second, at least 67 ms apart.
  - **Year page turns** (`RoomAudio.flipPage`): a page turns as each year starts to flip over on the counter (seven from 2026), forward only. The sound is "Singular Page Turn" by Flem0527 (Freesound #630019, CC0): one thin Bible page, light and crisp. It runs through the clock's path (its dive low-pass and room), and the ticks dip to half for a moment under it. The last year, 2019, turns a little lower and heavier (rate 0.86, louder): arrived. There is at most one turn every 0.12 s. Verified in Chrome (with the 2025 start): six turns through the spin, the last one lower.

## Performance (2026-11-19)

Goal: no dropped frames and no freezes, anywhere in the scroll. `scripts/check-portfolio-room-perf.mjs` is the audit. It needs a headless Chrome on the GPU with no vsync and no frame cap, because with vsync headless Chrome reports a flat 60 fps whatever the GPU load. Measured on an M1 Pro, 1440×900 at a pixel ratio of 1.75 (a retina laptop), warm, frame rate uncapped.

- **Shadow maps only where they show** (`shadowOn` at the end of `lights()` in `RoomScene.tsx`). Each spot was rendered with a map's shadow and without, and the pixels compared:
  - **The lamp's cube map** (six depth passes a frame) changes 14–21 % of the pixels in his room, and at most 0.17 % anywhere else. In the hall the lamp is at zero, yet its passes still drew the classmates.
  - **The sun's map** changes nothing in his room or at his desk (the sun is at zero there), and 4–53 % in the hall, the office and the classroom.

  So the lamp's map is drawn only in his room (and the arena), and the sun's only outside the room while it is lit. Where a map is not drawn, its shadow intensity is 0, so a stale map never shows. Both lights keep `castShadow`, so no shader recompiles; checked: no programs are created on a scroll through. The picture differs from both maps on by at most 0.17 % of pixels, with a mean of 0.2/255. Back in the room, it is identical.

  | Section | fps before | fps after |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | desk | 44 | 74 |
  | office | 55 | 91 |
  | mail | 57 | 79 |
  | grad | 63 | 112 |
  | school | 76 | 90 |
  | classroom | 114 | 130 |
  | room | 69 | 72 (map kept) |

  Before, the office, the desk and the mail missed 60 fps on this laptop.
- **Lamp map size: 1024 a cube face** (was 2048). Its six faces share one texture, so 2048 held 8192 × 4096 texels: 268 MB of GPU memory with its depth, a third of the page's. 1024 and 512 made no measurable difference to speed. The map's cost is the geometry drawn into it (2.2 M triangles in the room, 2.0 M of them his: the body 482 k in three faces, plus the socks and slippers, which have no culling, in all six), not its pixels. At 1024 his room's picture changes by 0.14–0.3 % of its pixels (mean 0.08/255). Side by side, the floor and window shadows look the same.
- **`FrameBudget.tsx`, adaptive resolution.** The frame's cost is mostly per pixel. His room runs 72 fps at a pixel ratio of 1.75, 88 at 1.5, 105 at 1.25 and 130 at 1. So when a quarter of the frames in a 1.5 s window are over 20 ms, the ratio is lowered:
  - It goes as far as the frames' mean says it takes, aiming at 14 ms (cost taken as the ratio squared, rounded down to a quarter, at least one step), and again if that was not enough.
  - Windows with a one-off frame (over 120 ms: a place mounting, the tab returning) are not judged. Nothing is judged for 3 s after the loader lifts.
  - It never raises the ratio: under vsync there is no reading the headroom, so it cannot flip-flop.
  - The value goes back through the Canvas's `dpr` prop, because the Canvas sets its prop again whenever the page re-renders.
  - Verified in the production build. At 1440×900 the ratio stays 1.75, cold and warm. At 2560×1440 (a 5K display) it steps 1.75 → 1.25 → 1 within the room's first seconds, and every section is over 70 fps after that.
- **First-visit freezes: every place in and drawn once behind the loader** (stage 2). Before, on a first visit each place's arrival froze the scroll: the office's and the classroom's mounting, and each place's first frame compiling its shader programs and uploading its textures and buffers. Measured in production, cache cleared: 13–14 frames over 100 ms per scroll through, up to 1.9 s (the office), 1.5 s (the classroom), 1.0 s (the hall), and 71–79 programs compiled on the way. Now:
  - **Every place mounts from the start.** The office and the classroom no longer wait for the story to near them (`RoomScene`). The classroom's model is preloaded too. Their building (the crowds, the city) happens behind the loader. The arena, out of the flow, still comes in at his desk.
  - **One light setup everywhere.** The hall's group was also shown in his room (never seen there, 0 pixels differ). Its speaker's light made four point lights in the room and three in every other place, so every material seen in both (his, the bag's) compiled twice. Now the hall shows only in the hall, and every place has the same lights.
  - **The warm-up** (`RoomScene`, a frame callback after the story's own). From its first frame, before anything is drawn, every program of what is in already is sent to compile (2026-11-25, see Loading). Then, once every place is in:
    1. Every material's program is sent to compile at once (`gl.compile`). The browser compiles them in parallel while the frames go on, and each frame checks whether all are ready, up to 8 s. (three's own `compileAsync` threw on a material without a program.)
    2. Each place is drawn once, a frame each. Everything in it is shown and unculled, with the shadow map the place uses: the lamp's in his room, the sun's elsewhere. With both maps forced on, the hall's crowd went six times into the lamp's cube, 40 M triangles in one frame. That makes the GPU pipelines and uploads every texture and buffer. Whatever holds a light keeps its visibility, so the lights stay the place's own.
    3. A GPU fence, checked each frame and never waited on. The loader lifts once it has signalled. Without it, the draws returned at once and the work they set off landed in the story's first seconds as 0.2–0.6 s freezes.
  - **The shadow passes' depth material.** three draws every shadow caster with one shared depth material, its side set per object, and picks its program again only when something else changes. So which side's program it needed, and when, depended on the drawing order: one compiled mid-chapter 1, a 0.12 s freeze. During the warm-up each caster's `onBeforeShadow` marks it for update, so all of them compile there.
  - **The loader** (`RoomPortfolio`) waits for the warm-up (`onReady`); its percentage goes on through it (see Loading). `FrameBudget` judges only once it has lifted, so the warm-up's frames are not taken for a slow GPU.
  - **The office's own precompile is gone.** It compiled for whatever lights were shown at the time.
  - **The city's and the interviewers' frame work** checked only their own parent's visibility, which is always visible, so it ran while the office was hidden. They now check every group above (`shown.ts`).

  - **His body, simplified** (`scripts/build-hero-lod.mjs`, `heroLod.ts`).
    - **Why.** At 482 k triangles, his body was the costliest single thing in every frame, skinned once for the frame and again in each shadow pass. It was skinned whole, from the source mesh, so that his face kept its detail (`build_web_glb.py`).

    - **Checked side by side.** In close-ups his face, hands and hoodie look the same at 30 %. At 10 % the hoodie's texture smeared on a sleeve, so 10 % is used for shadows only. Shadows drawn from the 10 % level instead of the 30 % one change at most 0.015 % of the pixels.
    - **The file** is 515 KB: meshopt-coded index buffers, triangles in vertex-cache order. It records each part's vertex count, triangle count and index hash. A part that doesn't match the model loaded keeps its full mesh and logs why, and so does every part when the file is missing.
    - **Upkeep.** Run the script again after rebuilding the GLB. In development, `?hero=full` draws him at full resolution for comparison.

- **Loading** (2026-11-25, the user's question: is the wait real?). It is: the loader waits for the models to come in and be read, the scene to be built, and the warm-up above.
  - **What comes in:** 14.0 MB over the wire (Vercel compresses it with brotli), 16.2 MB before. The models are most of it: his figure 4.7 MB, his room 2.4, the open office 2.4, the classroom 0.8, the hall 0.6, the window's photo layers 1.0.

Done with glTF-Transform's `draco()`: positions and UVs at 16 bits, normals at 12. By each mesh's size, nothing moves by more than 0.05 mm in his room or 0.3 mm in the hall (whose one mesh is 38 m across), and no UV by more than a tenth of a texel.
